Current master's research

PI–CNT–Metal Hybrid Conductors

Polymer-supported metal thin films · engineered carbon interlayers · electrical reliability

Metal films on compliant polymers can lose conductivity through cracking, delamination, and unstable film continuity. I investigate whether a buried CNT network can act as an engineered interphase that changes metal growth and failure—not merely as a parallel conductor after the metal breaks.

Research question
How do CNT topology and surface condition alter film continuity, stress redistribution, crack onset, and resistance evolution?
Current workflow
CNT film preparation and transfer → metal deposition → tensile/electrical testing → crack and morphology analysis.
Mechanism controls
PI/metal · PI/CNT/metal · PI/functionalized-CNT/metal · CNT-only reference
Next validation
Quantitative adhesion, cyclic reliability, thermal/environmental exposure, and correlation between morphology and ΔR/R₀.
CNT transferPVD / e-beam depositionTensile–resistance testingSEM / OMInterface mechanicsFlexible conductors

Central claim to test: the CNT layer changes the topology and failure mechanism of the metal film, beyond providing a redundant conductive path.

02
Active research direction

CNT Surface Chemistry & Metal-Film Affinity

Surface functionalization · wetting · nucleation · adhesion

I am developing a surface-chemistry framework that links CNT oxidation or functionalization to metal-film growth and interface reliability. The objective is to replace the vague idea of “affinity” with measurable quantities such as wettability, chemical state, nucleation density, film coverage, and interfacial strength.

Chemical analysis
Raman and FTIR interpretation; contact-angle analysis for wettability; advanced surface analysis to be added where available.
Growth linkage
Surface functionality → nucleation and coverage → interface morphology → cracking and resistance stability.
Scientific value
Separates true interface engineering from a simple increase in carbon content or total conductor thickness.
Target applications
Flexible interconnects, advanced packaging, lightweight wiring, sensors, and electronics for harsh environments.

CFD & Multiphysics for Materials Processing

Transport · heat transfer · reactor flow · process uniformity

My CFD experience gives me a quantitative foundation for transport-dominated problems. I aim to apply it to materials processing—particularly CVD/ALD reactor flow, precursor delivery, purge dynamics, thermal uniformity, and ultimately the coupling between processing history and interface quality.

Current capability

Materials-processing extension

Device-level extension

Research philosophy

Potential contribution

A researcher who can connect the interface to the system.

I can enter a project at the materials level—surface treatment, film growth, morphology, and adhesion—and carry the question through electrical failure, environmental reliability, and transport modeling.
